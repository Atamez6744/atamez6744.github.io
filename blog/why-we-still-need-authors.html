If AI Can Teach Us Almost Anything, Why Do We Still Need Authors?

November 22, 2026 · 3 min read

Something caught my attention in a few books I've read recently.

Authors are increasingly sharing how they used AI in their writing process. Some even go out of their way to reassure readers that the ideas and words are still theirs.

I find that transparency refreshing. But it also makes me wonder: What exactly are we afraid of losing?

Authenticity? Originality? The creative struggle? Or simply the human behind the words?

There's an interesting paradox here.

We now have AI tools that can explain, summarize, challenge, and personalize almost anything we want to learn. Yet we still seek out our favorite authors.

We want their stories. Their perspectives. Their experiences. Not just the information they have to share.

Maybe that's because knowledge is becoming abundant, but human perspective isn't interchangeable.

And as AI makes it easier than ever to turn a half-baked idea into a polished manuscript, I suspect we'll see more books that probably should have been articles. Or maybe even LinkedIn posts. 😂

But here's what I'm wrestling with.

Did AI help the author think more deeply, or did it simply make the writing easier?

And there's an even more fascinating question: What will we never get to read because AI touched the work in the first place?

Maybe the author would have struggled for weeks before arriving at an unexpected insight. Maybe they would have connected ideas that seemed completely unrelated. Or maybe AI helped them reach somewhere they never could have reached alone.

We'll never know. Once AI enters the creative process, the alternative version of that work becomes unknowable.

And whose concern should that be?

Is it up to the audience to decide whether the finished work moves them, regardless of how it was created? Or should authors themselves wonder about the ideas they might never discover because AI got there first?

Then there's music. That's where my feelings get complicated.

I want to believe music is inseparable from human experience. From heartbreak, joy, longing, and everything in between. But if an AI-generated song moves me to tears, does it matter that no human experienced the emotions behind its creation?

Maybe it comes down to what each of us values.

Which brings me to a prediction.

I suspect that as AI-generated content becomes abundant, genuinely human-created work may command a premium.

Not necessarily because it's technically better, but because we value knowing that someone lived, struggled, questioned, and made thousands of choices to create it.

Perhaps the future of creativity isn't simply about the quality of what gets produced, but about how much we value the human journey behind it.

And I keep coming back to two questions:

For readers: If a book profoundly changed your life, and you later discovered AI wrote 80% of it, would that diminish its value?

For authors: If AI helps you create something brilliant, do you ever wonder what you might have created without it?
