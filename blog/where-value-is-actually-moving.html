Where Value Is Actually Moving

July 15, 2026 · 3 min read

Here's some food for thought. AI isn't taking away the work. It's taking away how much of the work proves your value. When execution gets easier, judgment becomes visible, and suddenly everyone can see who's really steering the ship. AI is the truth serum.

The people who stand out right now can take a messy, tangled problem and turn it into something solvable. They weigh tradeoffs when there's no obvious answer. They tie decisions back to real business impact. And maybe hardest of all, they decide what matters next and what can wait. The prize is better decisions, and more output was never the point. What a time to be alive!

This is already happening inside the roles we have today. Take the project manager. AI can write status reports, track milestones, and organize tasks beautifully. Projects rarely fail for lack of tracking, though. They fail because decisions were fuzzy. So the project manager's value moves upstream: noticing a project drifting before the metrics catch it, getting stakeholders aligned when each one defines success differently, putting tradeoffs between scope, time, and quality on the table early, and protecting the outcome as fiercely as the timeline. It's a subtle shift. You're the system of judgment now, and the system of record can handle itself.

The solution architect is seeing the same thing. AI can sketch design options and even help with implementation. What it can't do is read context the way you do. Your value lives in framing the problem correctly before anyone starts building, weighing scalability against cost and complexity, spotting the downstream ripples others miss, and recommending a clear path when several "good" options are competing for attention. Building the solution is only part of the job. Choosing which solution deserves to be built is the real craft.

Zoom out and the pattern is clear. We're moving from doing the work to deciding which work matters. That's harder. It's less defined, more exposed, and at first glance, harder to measure.

So how do you prove judgment? Through outcomes. Dan Shipper puts it perfectly: "you won't be judged on how much you know, but on how well you can allocate and manage the resources of intelligence to get work done." Think of it like a conductor. Nobody asks the conductor to play every instrument. They judge the music. Your experience still matters. It just shows up differently now, in the calls you make, the work you point AI toward, and the results that follow. I repeat, what a time to be alive!
