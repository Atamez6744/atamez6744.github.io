Manic Monday: To Be Seen

August 15, 2026 · 3 min read

At the heart of every meaningful relationship is a simple human desire: to be seen.

Each of us arrives with a unique combination of code. Our DNA, experiences, predispositions, strengths, insecurities, preferences, and perspectives shape who we are and what we bring to the table. It is our offering, for better or worse. To be truly seen is to feel safe enough to bring that full self forward, imperfections included.

Being seen is not the same as being acknowledged.

It is being understood. It is having someone recognize not only what you do, but how you think. What energizes you. What challenges you. Where you thrive. Where you need support.

When someone truly sees you, they give you permission to operate from your strengths. They trust your judgment. They believe in your capability. At the same time, you trust them to help illuminate your blind spots and guide you in areas where their strengths exceed your own.

That mutual trust creates something powerful. It creates the psychological safety to experiment. To test ideas without fear of failure. To challenge assumptions. To ask questions. To be curious. To push beyond what is comfortable. It creates the conditions where creativity flourishes and growth becomes possible.

This is undoubtedly where our best work happens. When people have this, they shine.

Rarely is great work the product of individual talent alone. More often, it emerges from symbiotic relationships where people see each other clearly and leverage one another's strengths. One person's vision complements another's execution. One person's curiosity fuels another's expertise. Finding these relationships is one of the most important pursuits in life.

The people who truly see us expand who we can become.

Some of the people who have shaped my career most weren't on my team, and in some cases weren't even in my organization. They were the people who saw something in me before I fully saw it in myself. They challenged me when it would have been easier to agree. They pushed me to think bigger, lead better, and grow beyond what felt comfortable.

The longer I work, the more I believe that doing your best work is less about finding the perfect role and more about finding the right people. The ones who amplify your strengths, challenge your blind spots, and create the trust that allows you to flourish.

Find those people. Invest in those relationships. Learn from them. And become that person for someone else. Those people make all the difference.

"To be loved but not known is comforting but superficial. To be known and not loved is our greatest fear. But to be fully known and truly loved is, well, a lot like being loved by God. It is what we need more than anything."
