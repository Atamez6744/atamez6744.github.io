Take It All Away Then

November 24, 2026 · 3 min read

I've sat through fifty or sixty one-hour leadership seminars from top consulting firms, bestselling authors, and Fortune 500 leaders. Almost every one opens the same way. The facilitator asks the chat some version of one question: How are you feeling about all this change? How do you stay focused in the AI era? How do you feel about the future of work?

And most sessions, the chat fills up with the same words: Overwhelmed. Lost. No direction.

We've all felt that. But when I read closely, I hear something deeper than stress about new tools. I hear people saying, "If AI can do what I do, I lack meaning." That sentence deserves our full attention. For some, or for many, a job title became a soul.

We built a culture that celebrates success, and then we defined success as output, titles, and paychecks. So when a machine starts producing the output, the whole identity starts to wobble. The fear is real, I've felt it. And underneath the fear is an old truth waiting to be remembered: you are more than your job.

When exactly did we start measuring our worth like machines measure theirs? By output. How much did I produce today? How fast? How well? AI is now beating us at that game, and maybe that's the gift. Because we were never built for that paradigm. We were created to feel, to love, to experience, to chase awe and adventure, to create and express. Your value was never in your output. It was in your aliveness all along.

So here's the question I want to ask in the next seminar chat. Take it all away then. Your title, your inbox, your tasks. If AI took your job tomorrow, who would still be standing there?

My mother raised me on a line I carry everywhere: "El que no vive para servir, no sirve para vivir." Whoever doesn't live to serve isn't fit to live. Service is the meaning that never automates. Your love for your people, your curiosity, your faith, saying yes to inconveniencing oneself, the way you show up for a neighbor. No model can take that, and it pays dividends.

Leaders, governments, and companies have real work to do here: equip people, invest in skills, tell the truth about what's changing. And each of us holds way more agency than what we think. You choose to keep learning. You choose where your meaning lives. You choose a life bigger than work.

AI can produce. Only you can live, feel, absorb, and create in many ways beyond tokens. So, who are you? Really?
