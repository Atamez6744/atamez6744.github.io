Happiness Is Only Real When Shared

November 21, 2026 · 3 min read

Tonight, I took myself out to dinner. No plus one. No birthday. No special occasion on the calendar. Just me, celebrating a little act of courage that felt worth celebrating.

And I went all out.

Crab mac and cheese, rich, golden, and fabulous. A burger with sprinkle fries that made me grin like a little kid. A glass of wine more expensive than I'd normally order, worth every sip. And to finish, chocolate lava cake with vanilla ice cream and raspberries.

I savored every bite. Read between courses. Got a little work done. Took my sweet time.

And somewhere between the wine and dessert, I thought about my dad.

Growing up, money was tight. My parents had five daughters, and feeding seven people was no small feat. I remember family trips to the grocery store, all of us walking the aisles together. My dad at the checkout counter, carefully watching the numbers add up. He was actually the less frugal one compared to my mom, but even he knew there were limits.

Sometimes we had to put things back. Sometimes we couldn't get everything we wanted.

But we always left together.

It's funny what stays with you.

I've traveled through Mexico, the United States, Costa Rica, Nicaragua, Guatemala, Honduras, and El Salvador. Through mission work, I've met people with very little who somehow found so much to give.

And I've come to believe that abundance has very little to do with how much you own.

There's a line from Into the Wild that has stayed with me for years: "Happiness only real when shared." Christopher McCandless traveled into the wilderness searching for freedom, only to discover something my family had been teaching me all along.

You can have very little and feel like you have everything.

Matthew 16:26 asks what good it is to gain the whole world if you lose your soul.

And tonight, sitting alone at a beautiful restaurant, enjoying a meal I once wouldn't have imagined ordering without checking the price, I felt incredibly grateful. Not just for the dinner. Or for the freedom to celebrate myself. But for the memories that came rushing back with it.

The grocery aisles. My dad watching the total. My sisters beside me. The things we left behind and the love we always took home.

Maybe that's one of life's sweetest surprises. Sometimes the little things we celebrate today take us back to the things that mattered most all along.

So celebrate the little wins. Order dessert. Raise a glass to yourself (or two). Take the time to appreciate how far you've come.

And don't forget the people who made the journey worth it. Recordar es vivir.
