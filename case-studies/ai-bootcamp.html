The 10-Day AI Bootcamp

How one short email a day took people from "I opened Copilot once" to treating AI like a teammate, and spread across Applied Materials by word of mouth.

The Challenge

Everyone had Copilot. Hardly anyone had a teammate. People typed a question into a blank window, got something generic back, and decided AI "wasn't that useful for my job."

The gap wasn't access, and it wasn't features. It was habit. Nobody had shown people the obvious first steps that make everything else work, and a one-hour training session, however good, rarely survives the rest of the week.

The Approach: Onboard AI Like a New Hire

The bootcamp was built around one idea: you don't learn AI like software. You onboard it like a brilliant new hire who knows nothing about you yet. Inspired by Jeremy Utley's Gen AI Bootcamp, I rebuilt the model for our people, our tools, and our work: one short email a day, one drill, one thing to try before lunch.

It starts with what everyone skips

Day 1 isn't a prompt trick. It's introducing yourself: setting up your instructions and telling the tool who you are, what you do, and what great looks like in your role. It's so obvious that almost everyone skips it, and it quietly changes every answer that comes after. Hyper-personalizing the tool to you is the difference between a generic chatbot and a teammate who already knows your work.

The people who get the most from AI are not the ones with the best technology. They are the ones who invest three minutes in context.

— From Day 1 of the bootcamp

Then one new muscle a day

Each day builds on the one before it. Here's the shape of the ten days and the gist of each day's homework:

01
Introduce yourself
Set up your instructions. Make the tool know you.
02
Never take the first answer
Push back, regenerate, ask for three more.
03
Context is the skill
Brief it the way you'd brief a new hire.
04
Let it interview you
Flip it around: have AI ask you the questions.
05
The synthesizer
Turn what you've avoided reading into actions.
06
The honest critic
Ask it to find the holes in your own work.
07
Your Team of Five
Give AI five roles and let them debate your problem.
08
Stop repeating yourself
Turn your best prompt into something reusable.
09
Move up the matrix
Hand over one valuable weekly task, not a trivial one.
10
Show and tell
Share what you built and teach one person how.

The full prompts, demos, and exercises stay inside the bootcamp.

Every email followed the same rhythm: a short story, one concept, a 10-minute exercise, and an open invitation to reply with questions. On the days where seeing it beat reading about it, I added a short video demo.

How It Spread

I never made it mandatory, and it never went on a training calendar. People who signed up told their coworkers, and those coworkers reached out to join. Forty-eight people signed up that way.

That's the signal I trust most. People don't recommend something to their colleagues unless it actually helped them.

The Result

  • 48 organic sign-ups, all through word of mouth, with no mandate or assignment
  • Questions kept coming in while it ran, a sign people were trying the drills in their real work and wanted to go further
  • The video demos got watched on the days that had them
  • Thank-you messages when it wrapped up

Most people don't need more AI features. They need someone to show them the obvious thing they skipped, and then give them a reason to come back tomorrow.

— Ale Tamez
